About

Bruno Cornelio

I work out what a product has to do, and where AI belongs in it. Then I get it built.

At a product consultancy, I started a new discovery about every three weeks, and not always in a field I knew: cannabis retail, farm programmes, a university, a security product. I took part in about 40 of them, in more than nine industries. The job each time: sit with the people who do the work, and turn what they know into rules, flows and a first release a team can build.

My work doesn’t stop at the handover. I direct AI agents that write the code, and I decide what ships. One of those builds is an offline safety registry for a hospital pharmacy in Lima: a small AI model reads the handwritten forms, and a person confirms every field.

That is how I use AI in general. Many teams think AI means a chatbot, and try to fit it into every step. I look for the step where it beats the alternatives: a person, a simple rule or plain code. Then I give it a job, a target and a limit.

AI where it beats the alternative. Not everywhere.

02

How I work with people

I catch edge cases and I keep things simple. In practice:

  1. 01

    I bring a prototype to the first interview.

    Even when I’m new to the field. It’s deliberately simple, so people react to something concrete. Sometimes it’s far from what people need, and that’s the point: we find out early.

  2. 02

    Before I say no, I show the trade-off.

    When a request needs a lot of effort, I put a simpler build next to it: less work, similar value, faster learning. Side by side, clients see why one thing is in and another is out. It’s easy to fall in love with an idea before anyone knows it works.

  3. 03

    I fix the problem first. Then the process.

    When something breaks, I don’t start with who caused it. We solve it before it grows, then I find the step in the process that failed. If a person made the mistake, that’s human: they get a clear way to use what they learned.

  4. 04

    I say what’s true, and I stay friendly.

    I’m direct when it’s needed. I don’t turn it into a fight.
