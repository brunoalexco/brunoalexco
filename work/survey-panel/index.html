01

Problem

A market-research panel pays people in points for answering surveys, and an LLM follows up on their answers in conversation to draw out more. Every point converts to a gift card. The researchers running the panel needed rules that pay for useful information, show which respondents are worth asking again and keep the rewards from outrunning the money behind them.

I was the product manager at Valere on this client. The app screens had been designed and an earlier discovery had fallen short before I joined. The client had a rough idea of what it wanted; the logic behind the screens was mine to write.

02

The system

A respondent sees points, a multiplier and a wheel. What decides how any of it behaves sits on the researcher’s side and in the rules between the two, and that is the part I wrote.

03

Key decision

The call
Drive the reward multiplier with one logarithmic curve set by two global parameters, a maximum and a steepness.
Alternatives
Define 30 or more discrete milestones, each with its own information threshold.
Why
Each step up the curve asks for more new information than the last, so points follow what a respondent actually tells the panel. Operators retune the whole economy by changing two values, where milestones would mean maintaining dozens of thresholds.
04

What I specified

I wrote the logic into the feature sheet row by row, for the app and for the admin portal: the variables, the question types, the in-app actions and the LLM survey settings. I worked it out from how comparable apps behave and from my own reasoning, then presented it and answered the questions it raised.

The task rules

my design

What decides when a task shows, when it stops and what it pays.

  • Six ways a task can start

    at a date and time, the same for everyoneat a date and time on the user's own clockwhen another task is completedwhen the home page is emptywhen a reward balance crosses a thresholdwhen time has passed since another task

    An LLM follow-up is a task whose start is the end of a survey.

  • Four ways a task ends

    the sample size is reachedthe points threshold is hitit sat unopened or unfinished too longthe close date arrives

  • The first survey comes first

    When
    A person has just registered
    Then
    One survey is active, and nothing else opens until it is done

  • No finish, no reward

    When
    A survey or a conversation is left incomplete
    Then
    It pays nothing

  • A weak answer is asked again

    When
    The respondent answers the LLM
    If
    The backend finds the answer short of what the task expects
    Then
    The model asks them to clarify

  • Deleting a person keeps the research

    When
    A user deletes their account
    Then
    Name, login, contact details and password are cut off

    Their surveys and conversations stay, under an anonymous numeric ID.

The economy

my design

Points are money here, so the formulas are in the product and not in a spreadsheet beside it. Names are the sheet's own.

  • One curve, two settings

    When
    A respondent climbs a multiplier step
    Then
    The step costs more new information the higher they already are

    1.15× sits at 3.90 units, 1.35× at 9.62that step costs 9.62 − 3.90 = 5.72 new units

  • Decay

    When
    A day passes
    Then
    The multiplier slides toward 1×, faster the higher it is

    One setting says how much a maxed multiplier loses in 24 hours. The rest is interpolated, and 1× does not decay.

  • The debt forecast

    OutstandingDollarObligations(t) = OutstandingDollarObligations(t−1) + EstimatedPointsAvailableForRedemption(t) × LikelihoodOfDailyRedemption(t) × ExpectedConversionRate(t)

    Run in a normal and a worst case. When the forecast passes the funds available, the admin gets a liquidity alert.

  • Is the sample on track?

    RequiredCompletionRate(t) = (TargetSample − SampleSize(t)) / (DeadlineDay − t)

    Set against the actual daily rate. An alert fires when the sample is met, or will not be.

  • What a spin costs the panel

    DollarImpactPerSpin(t) = (PointsAwarded(t) − PointsDeducted(t)) × ConversionRate(t) / TotalSpins(t)

    Three spins a day, never stacked.

  • Who is worth asking

    quality = 50% test-question accuracy + 25% LLM conversion rate + 25% LLM information score

    Beside it sit responsiveness, the first day a person is at least 50% likely to have finished a survey, and cost in points per minute.

Scope in four states. Every row carries V1, V2, V3 or DELETE, so deferred and removed are different outcomes. Inside compound conditions, colour-coded sub-rows mark which options ship first. For a research panel the survey, conversation and analytics engine is the product, so nearly the whole core loop stayed in the first version.

Deferred scope. 2-factor auth, a trophy case and wheel-physics tuning sit in V2.

05

AI design choices

The LLM runs the follow-up conversation. The rules around it belong to the admin.

  • What it may draw on. For each conversation task the admin sets the system prompt and API parameters, pairs a goal scope with a topical scope and ticks which sources the model may use: survey answers, demographics, previous surveys, earlier conversations and location.
  • What it may not decide. Points and the multiplier come from the curve. The threshold that closes a task is a financial limit set outside the model.
  • A check on every response. Each answer is evaluated in the backend, and one that falls short makes the LLM ask the respondent to clarify.
  • How quality is measured. The dashboard tracks a funnel: the share of people who start the follow-up, then the share who give X, Y and Z units of information. The same measures feed each person’s quality score, so conversation quality decides who is asked next.
  • Where the model choice stood. The client had tested GPT-4o and Claude Sonnet 3.5 on a real survey to see how each would engage a respondent. The prompt settings in the sheet build on that material.
