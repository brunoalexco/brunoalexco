01

Problem

An agricultural sustainability program that works with farms across the United States and Canada was replacing its legacy reporting platform, where farmers, their advisors and program staff record field boundaries, practice data and signed agreements. The replacement was mid-build, with much of the scope decided, a quarter-end launch commitment and a roles model that did not yet hold together.

02

My role

Product manager at Valere on the build: I owned the backlog, requirements, prioritization recommendations and design coordination with the developers. The client’s product owner kept final authority on business rules, legal questions and the launch.

03

The system

A farmer’s record passes through several hands before it is locked, and the map under it draws on public datasets with their own limits. I joined with most of this designed; the parts marked are the flows I refined and wrote stories for.

04

Key decision

The call
Give users who are both Advisor and Delegate a role switch, not a compound role
Alternatives
  1. A compound Advisor-plus-Delegate role with merged permissions
  2. A third role state alongside the existing ones
Why
It was the simplest of the three designs on the table. Signing is binding and only some roles may sign, so keeping each session in one role keeps every permission and signing rule unambiguous for the developers and for users.
05

Approach

  • Learning an inherited product. I started with the legacy platform, which held every requirement being asked for, and with onboarding sessions with the client. Then I turned the field-boundary, invitation, role and signing flows into epics and stories with acceptance criteria and walked the developers through them in Figma before they went to Jira.
  • Two proposals of my own. For the map: let a farmer click a boundary that is already in the public dataset, in place of drawing every field by hand. For onboarding: a shorter invitation flow. Some of my proposals were accepted and built.
  • Testing the roles model live. In a session with the backend developers I asked what happens when a user holds only one role. That showed the system as scoped did not support one delegate and many advisors per farmer, and we corrected it on the call.
  • Deciding what data comes across. With the backend developer I worked through what the migration carries, and how a past year’s data point can be corrected while the value a modeled outcome was based on is kept.
  • A notification inventory for launch. I listed the 316 notifications the platform could send, each with its trigger, recipient role and channel, and tagged each one launch, launch stretch, deferred or out of scope, so the client’s product owner and subject expert could choose.
  • A bridge between two teams. The new build and the legacy system sat with developer teams in two countries. I was asked to connect them, and took the migration questions to the build team’s lead. With the client’s product owner I also built a one-page guide to the cadence: one story through every stage.
  • Planning ahead of the sprint. When decisions were holding up the build, I moved design thinking to three or four weeks before implementation.
  • Asking for the problem before the AI. When the client’s external advisor suggested using AI to ingest farmers’ data from spreadsheets in many formats, I said there are many ways to apply AI and it is not always generative AI, asked for a written problem statement before proposing tools, and raised it again weeks later when it had gone quiet.

Roles and signing

flows I refined

  • One delegate, up to ten advisors

    When
    A farmer invites someone
    If
    The role's limit is not reached: one delegate, ten advisors
    Then
    The invitation goes out

  • Who may sign

    When
    A signature is submitted
    If
    The session's role is farmer or delegate
    Then
    It is stored with who signed
    Else
    The backend refuses it with a 403

  • One role per session

    When
    A person is both advisor and delegate
    Then
    They work in one role at a time and switch with one control

    Permissions and signing stay unambiguous.

  • A disabled account is not deleted

    When
    An admin disables a user
    Then
    All access is revoked and the audit trail stays

Fields and data

  • An associated area is not a new field

    When
    A farmer adds a pollinator strip or a cut-out
    If
    It does not overlap another associated area
    Then
    It is stored as a polygon linked to its parent field

  • Warn or block

    When
    Entered data fails a check
    If
    The check is a hard blocker
    Then
    Submission stops
    Else
    A soft warning shows and the farmer may go on

  • Only what the first version uses is migrated

    When
    Legacy data is moved to the new build
    Then
    Data for features that exist in the first version comes across
    Else
    The rest stays on the backend as legacy fields, for later

  • Three years of crop layers

    When
    A new annual crop layer arrives
    Then
    It joins a rolling window of three years

What I recommended against. A compound Advisor-plus-Delegate role and a third role state. The existing role switch covers the dual-role case.

06

Outcome

Close to the launch date, open bugs meant a full launch would have undermined users’ confidence. I co-owned the move to a controlled, production-ready interim environment and committed to re-triage the open bug list with the delivery lead the next business day, so only working features would go forward. My work on the project ended in June 2026, before the launch.
